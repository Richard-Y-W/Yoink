# Yoink Drop Art Pack - Research And Style Guide

## Purpose

This pack defines the first collectible item world for Yoink Market. The goal is to make drops feel like limited toy-shop releases: unique, playful, collectible, and transparent about rarity and stock.

The first art set has 16 hero items across four families:

- Pocket Tech
- Holo Finds
- Desk Pets
- Snack Relics

## Research Guardrails

Scarcity can increase purchase intent when users understand the value and limits. A 2024 NFT study found that perceived scarcity can support purchase intention, while perceived risk works against it: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2024.104424

Blind-box and uncertainty research shows that surprise can create curiosity, but it can also push instant gratification and gambler's fallacy. Yoink should avoid paid random pulls as the core mechanic and use visible rarity, visible edition counts, and honest stock instead: https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-025-02644-w

Fake scarcity is a deceptive pattern. Yoink drops should use real counts, clear release windows, and honest sold-out states: https://deceptive.design/types/fake-scarcity/

## Core Art Direction

Yoink items should feel like cartoon collectible shop objects:

- chunky rounded shapes
- thick dark outlines
- soft vinyl or clay lighting
- pastel body colors with one bold accent
- sticker-like details
- tiny face or personality detail when it fits the item
- rarity effects that support the object instead of hiding it

The render should feel like a small physical toy photographed in a clean digital studio, not like a flat icon. Use a simple front three-quarter angle, centered composition, generous padding, and a clean pastel background.

Avoid:

- real brand logos
- exact references to existing protected characters
- readable fake text
- realistic grime or horror styling
- overly complex backgrounds
- dark moody lighting
- casino, gambling, or loot-box language

## Rarity Visual Language

Common:
- clean matte toy finish
- one clear silhouette
- no special particle effects

Uncommon:
- small charm, sticker badge, or accent piece
- slightly richer material contrast

Rare:
- foil edge, glossy accent, or shiny trim
- stronger color contrast

Ultra Rare:
- subtle glow, shimmer rim, or iridescent material
- still readable at small card size

One-Off:
- odd colorway
- tiny serial mark or signature stamp shape
- one unmistakable special trait

## Family Notes

### Pocket Tech

Nostalgic electronics reimagined as soft vinyl desk toys. The forms should be instantly readable: CRT, flip phone, MP3 player, toy camera. Use rounded corners, little buttons, tiny screens, sticker marks, and friendly faces.

### Holo Finds

Collectible card and sticker culture without copying real card games. These should feel shiny, slabbed, foil-stamped, and display-worthy. Use abstract creatures, icons, tickets, pucks, and sticker forms instead of real brands.

### Desk Pets

Mascot-style objects with the strongest character potential. These should be simple, memorable, and easy to turn into recurring Yoink characters. Use plush/vinyl surfaces, simple eyes, tiny props, and compact silhouettes.

### Snack Relics

Prize toys and vending-machine treasures. These should feel like strange objects pulled from cereal boxes, capsule machines, or crinkle packs. Keep them playful and collectible, not food-realistic.

## App Usage

Recommended first use inside Yoink:

- Home drop banner: feature one family per drop
- Listing cards: use the render, rarity badge, edition count, stock left
- Product detail: larger render, item traits, drop lore, edition number
- Watching: save items and drops
- Orders: show item thumbnail through delivery stages

## Render Format

The render files in this pack are concept renders, not final production sprites. They should be used to guide the Yoink `ItemArt` system and future generated/hand-polished assets.

Target final app usage:

- square item art
- 1024px source size preferred
- clean item centered on a pastel background
- item readable at 96px card thumbnail size
